Now what does that mean?

For research

  • Code every message, not just the first. Most follow-ups added detail the first message left out (87%), and about half asked something new (47%).
  • Few people ask about a drug by name; only 12% asked about a specific medication, even though the AI named one in 22 of 56 chats.

For healthcare & pharma

  • Patients ask AI what is wrong first. Half asked what was causing a symptom, and every one of them was pointed to a professional (vs. 58% of others).
  • Treatment questions lean toward treatment answers. People who asked how to manage something got a medication suggestion more often (38% vs. 15%) and a referral less often (62% vs. 88%); neither gap was significant.

Where the risk sits

  • People ask about their own bodies (78%), and 1 in 10 asks for someone else, such as a partner or child. Only 9% of AI replies included a disclaimer.
  • Few people push back. Only 4 of 30 multi-message chats corrected or questioned the AI's answer.

Notes: exploratory pulse; 50 participants whose pasted conversation included their own messages. Group sizes are small, so differences are directional. BH-adjusted p-values are shown where tests were run. Question types, health area, who the question was about and follow-up behavior were coded from participant messages; referral and medication codes from the AI text. Chat excerpts are trimmed, with spelling lightly corrected and identifying details left out.
