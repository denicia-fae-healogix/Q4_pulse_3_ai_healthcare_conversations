# Pilot codebooks: first messages about health

Two separate codebooks are applied to the same message.

## Codebook 1: Health topic

**Rule.** Give every message exactly **one primary topic**: the main health subject. Add **one secondary topic** only if the message clearly also asks about a second subject. If the message has no health subject (for example a greeting or a one-word reply), or its subject fits none of the topics below, the primary topic is **NONE**.

Code the subject only, not what kind of answer the person wants.

### T1. Bones, joints and muscles

**Definition.** The message asks about pain, injury, or a condition of the bones, joints, muscles, back, or neck. Includes knee, back, neck, foot, and elbow pain, arthritis, osteoporosis, bone density and fracture risk, and bony lumps.

**Signs in the message:**

- names a joint, limb, back, or neck as the site of pain or injury
- mentions arthritis, osteoarthritis, osteoporosis, fractures, or bone density (DEXA) results
- pain after walking, exercise, lifting, or an injury
- asks what kind of joint or muscle injury something is

**Close call, vs. Headaches and nerve pain:** Bones, joints and muscles covers pain arising from bones, joints, muscles, and the spine. Headaches and nerve pain covers headaches and pain described as nerve-related (for example neuropathy). If the message names neuropathy or nerve pain, use Headaches and nerve pain even if the pain is in a limb; if it describes joint, back, or muscle pain without naming nerves, use Bones, joints and muscles. Chest and abdominal pain are not part of this code.

**Examples:**

- "what kind of knee injury does this sound like"
- "i developed a sharp pain in my neck that raidates to my shoulders suddenly"
- "symptoms of osteoarthrits"

### T2. Diet, weight and fitness

**Definition.** The message asks about nutrition, body weight, exercise, or fitness goals. Includes healthy eating, protein and supplements for fitness, normal or ideal weight, fat loss, workouts, and weight-loss medicines such as GLP-1 drugs.

**Signs in the message:**

- asks about healthy foods, meal plans, or protein intake
- asks about normal or ideal weight, or losing fat or weight
- mentions workouts, exercise, or fitness goals
- mentions a GLP-1 or weight-loss medicine such as Zepbound

**Close call, vs. Diabetes and hormones:** Diet, weight and fitness is about lifestyle goals around eating, weight, and exercise. Diabetes and hormones is about a diagnosed or suspected hormone or metabolic condition (diabetes, A1c, cholesterol, hormone imbalance). If the message is about diet or supplements to treat a named condition, code the condition as primary and this code as an optional secondary.

**Examples:**

- "I want to discover affordable, healthy foods that will help me achieve my fitness and wellness goals."
- "What is the normal weight for a male who is 28 and five feet nine inches tall?"
- "What impact (if any) has taking a GLP-1A medication had on your physical health?"

### T3. Stomach and digestion

**Definition.** The message asks about the stomach, bowel, anus, or other parts of the digestive tract. Includes stomach aches, belly or abdominal pain, gastritis, and anal or perianal problems such as fistulas.

**Signs in the message:**

- stomach ache, tummy ache, or belly pain
- names a digestive condition such as gastritis
- pain or symptoms when using the bathroom
- anal or perianal problems such as a fistula or perianal abscess

**Close call, vs. Infections and acute illness:** Stomach and digestion is about a problem located in the digestive tract. Infections and acute illness is about sudden, whole-body illness or infection in general (fever, chills, body aches). If the main subject is a gut or anal problem, use Stomach and digestion even if infection is mentioned (an infection may be a secondary topic); if the message is about feeling generally ill or about infections in general, use Infections and acute illness. Weight and diet goals belong to Diet, weight and fitness.

**Examples:**

- "I've been dealing with some bad stomach aches for the past few weeks."
- "Could it be gastritis? Tell me what causes gastritis and how long it can last."

### T4. Skin, hair and allergies

**Definition.** The message asks about the skin, scalp, or hair, or about allergic reactions. Includes eczema, hives, flaking or scabbing scalp, hair growth or loss, and allergies to foods or medicines such as antibiotics.

**Signs in the message:**

- names a skin condition such as eczema, hives, or a rash
- scalp flaking or scabbing, or hair growth or hair loss
- asks about an allergic reaction or allergy to a food or drug

**Close call, vs. Infections and acute illness:** Skin, hair and allergies covers surface skin and hair problems and allergic reactions. Infections and acute illness covers infections and short-term whole-body illness. An abscess or infection under the skin goes to Infections and acute illness (or the affected organ system); a rash, hives, or allergic reaction goes here. Hair changes while on a weight-loss drug go here, with Diet, weight and fitness as an optional secondary topic.

**Examples:**

- "What does it mean if I have eczema dry spots on my skin"
- "My scalp is flaking and scabbing"
- "Can an allergic reaction appear slowly"

### T5. Infections and acute illness

**Definition.** The message asks about an infection or a sudden, short-term illness. Includes flu-like or cold symptoms (fever, chills, body aches, congestion), named infectious diseases such as hand foot and mouth disease, and questions about whether something is an infection.

**Signs in the message:**

- sudden onset of feeling unwell with fever, chills, or body aches
- asks what illness they caught or whether it is the flu
- names an infectious disease or asks about infections in general
- asks about an abscess or whether something is infected

**Close call, vs. Stomach and digestion:** Infections and acute illness is about infection or sudden whole-body illness. If an infection is clearly located in one body system that has its own code (for example a perianal abscess), use that system's code as primary and this code as secondary, or vice versa depending on which the message centres on. A headache on its own goes to Headaches and nerve pain; a headache that is one of several flu-like symptoms goes here. Tiredness that is part of a sudden flu-like illness goes here, not to Sleep and fatigue.

**Examples:**

- "Today I suddenly started feeling bad, fatigue, body aches, felt hot, later had the chills, nose is congested. What did I catch?"
- "What if hand foot and mouth isn't treated"
- "can infections of some sort smell like onions"

### T6. Diabetes and hormones

**Definition.** The message asks about diabetes, blood sugar, or another hormone or metabolic condition. Includes A1c results, diabetes, hormone imbalance, seeing an endocrinologist, testosterone, and cholesterol.

**Signs in the message:**

- mentions diabetes, blood sugar, or an A1c value
- asks about hormone imbalance or an endocrinologist
- mentions testosterone or high cholesterol as a condition to manage

**Close call, vs. Pregnancy and reproductive health:** Diabetes and hormones covers hormone and metabolic conditions in general. Pregnancy and reproductive health covers pregnancy, labor, periods, and female reproductive organs. PCOS can fit either: if the message centres on periods, fertility, or other reproductive conditions (such as PMDD or endometriosis), use Pregnancy and reproductive health; if it centres on hormones, blood sugar, or cholesterol, use this code; use the other as secondary when both are present.

**Examples:**

- "Would an endocrinologist benefit me from learning why my hormones are our of whack?"
- "what diabetes"

### T7. Sleep and fatigue

**Definition.** The message asks about sleep problems or ongoing tiredness. Includes poor or unrefreshing sleep, waking at night, sleep apnea, sleep loss, and fatigue, including sleep changes linked to a medicine.

**Signs in the message:**

- asks about sleep apnea or snoring
- waking during the night or trouble sleeping
- feeling tired even after enough sleep
- sleep changes after starting or changing a medicine

**Close call, vs. Infections and acute illness:** Sleep and fatigue covers sleep problems and tiredness as the main subject. If tiredness is one of several symptoms of a sudden flu-like illness, use Infections and acute illness. Stress, anxiety, and emotional struggles are not part of this code (see notes).

**Examples:**

- "I've been feeling unusually tired lately, even after getting a full night's sleep."
- "What is sleep apnea? I think I may have it."

### T8. Eyes and vision

**Definition.** The message asks about the eyes or eyesight. Includes double vision, glasses, cataract surgery, and visits to eye doctors or retina specialists.

**Signs in the message:**

- mentions vision changes such as double vision
- mentions glasses, an eye doctor, or a retina specialist
- asks about cataracts or eye surgery

**Close call, vs. Headaches and nerve pain:** Eyes and vision is about the eyes themselves. Pain behind the eyes that comes with a headache or general illness goes to Headaches and nerve pain (or Infections and acute illness), not here.

**Examples:**

- "Sometimes I'm not seeing double with my glasses on but sometimes I am."
- "My eye doctor is sending me to a retina specialist"

### T9. Headaches and nerve pain

**Definition.** The message asks about headaches or nerve pain. Includes headaches with or without other symptoms such as vomiting, and neuropathy or other pain described as coming from nerves.

**Signs in the message:**

- says they have a headache or asks what to take for one
- headache with vomiting or pain behind the eyes
- mentions neuropathy or nerve pain

**Close call, vs. Bones, joints and muscles:** Headaches and nerve pain covers headaches and pain named as nerve-related. Pain in a joint, muscle, back, or neck that is not described as nerve pain goes to Bones, joints and muscles. A headache that is one of several flu-like symptoms (fever, chills, body aches) goes to Infections and acute illness.

**Examples:**

- "I have a headache, what should I take?"
- "How to heal neuropathy pain in leg"

### T10. Pregnancy and reproductive health

**Definition.** The message asks about pregnancy, labor, menstruation, or the female reproductive organs. Includes labor and induction, periods, endometriosis, PCOS, and PMDD.

**Signs in the message:**

- mentions pregnancy, labor, or induction
- mentions periods or menstrual symptoms
- names a reproductive condition such as endometriosis, PCOS, or PMDD

**Close call, vs. Diabetes and hormones:** Pregnancy and reproductive health covers pregnancy and the female reproductive system. Diabetes and hormones covers hormone and metabolic conditions not tied to reproduction (diabetes, A1c, cholesterol, general hormone imbalance). When a message lists both kinds of conditions, code the one it centres on as primary and the other as secondary.

**Examples:**

- (no example shown)

### T11. Mental health and stress

**Definition.** The message is about the participant's mental or emotional wellbeing: stress, anxiety, low mood, or emotional struggles in relationships or family life, rather than a physical symptom.

**Signs in the message:**

- Words such as stressed, anxious, struggling, overwhelmed
- Describes feelings or relationships rather than a body part or physical symptom
- Asks how to cope or feel better emotionally

**Close call, vs. Sleep and fatigue:** Sleep and fatigue is about sleep problems or physical tiredness; Mental health and stress is about emotional strain, stress or anxiety, even if tiredness is mentioned.

**Examples:**

- "I just went a whole week without saying anything to my family."

## Codebook 2: Type of request

**Rule.** Give the message **every** request type that applies; one message can have several. If the message asks for nothing (for example a greeting, or a reply with no request content), the result is **NONE**. There is no code for replying to or continuing a conversation with the AI: if the message continues an earlier exchange, code only the request content it contains.

### R1. Ask what is causing symptoms

**Definition.** The participant describes their own or someone else's symptoms or health change and asks what could be causing it or what it means. The help wanted is an explanation or likely diagnosis for a specific personal situation.

**Signs in the message:**

- "What could be causing this?" or "what could be the problem"
- "What did I catch?" or "what kind of injury does this sound like"
- "Could it be X or Y?" offering candidate causes
- "Is that typical" or "Does that make sense?" about a described personal symptom
- "How do I know if I have X"

**Close call, vs. General health facts:** This code needs a personal case described in the message and a question about what is behind it. If the question is about a condition, medicine or procedure in general, use General health facts. A message can carry both (e.g. "Could it be gastritis? Tell me what causes gastritis"); code both.

**Examples:**

- "Sometimes I wake up feeling like I haven't rested at all. What could be causing this?"
- "nose is congested. What did I catch?"
- "what kind of knee injury does this sound like"

### R2. General health facts

**Definition.** The participant asks for general information about a condition, symptom, test, procedure, medicine or health measure, not tied to explaining their own case. This includes definitions, symptom lists, causes, norms, statistics and likely outcomes.

**Signs in the message:**

- "What is X?" or "symptoms of X"
- "Can X ...?" or "Does X ...?" general yes-or-no fact questions
- "List every ..." or "Percentage of people ..."
- "What if X isn't treated" or "what causes X and how long can it last"
- "What will they do" at a test or appointment

**Close call, vs. Ask what is causing symptoms:** General health facts asks how health works in general; Ask what is causing symptoms asks to explain a specific personal case. Decide by whether the question is about the person's own described symptoms (code R1) or about a named condition or topic in general (code R2); code both if both are asked.

**Examples:**

- "Can an allergic reaction appear slowly"
- "What is the normal weight for a male who is 28 and five feet nine inches tall?"

### R3. Ask what to do for relief

**Definition.** The participant asks what they (or the person they care for) can do, take or change to relieve, treat or manage a current health problem. The help wanted is practical self-care or treatment advice.

**Signs in the message:**

- "What should I take?"
- "What can I do to help/alleviate..."
- "How to heal X"
- "what do I do?" after describing a problem
- "Would switching ... help" about adjusting a current treatment

**Close call, vs. Whether to seek medical care:** This code asks for self-care, medicine or habit steps to ease a problem. If the only choice being weighed is whether or when to see, call or use a professional or procedure, use Whether to seek medical care. If the request is goal-driven (fitness, nutrition) rather than problem-driven, use Personal plan or recommendations.

**Examples:**

- "I have a headache, what should I take?"
- "what can i do to alleviate knee pain and get back to normal?"
- "How to heal neuropathy pain in leg"

### R4. Information shared without a question

**Definition.** The participant states a symptom, health detail or test result with no explicit question or request, leaving the AI to infer what help is wanted (usually an assessment). This includes bare symptom phrases and pasted reports or lab values.

**Signs in the message:**

- A short symptom phrase with no question, e.g. "CHEST PAIN WHEN RUNNING"
- A statement about someone's symptom or a noticed change, with no request
- Age plus a test value, e.g. "72 yo woman with 6.0 a1c"
- A pasted lab or imaging report with no request

**Close call, vs. Ask what is causing symptoms:** Use this code only when no question or request is stated. If the message explicitly asks what is causing it, what it is, or what to do, code that request instead.

**Examples:**

- "CHEST PAIN WHEN RUNNING"
- "Husband's low back started hurting yesterday"

### R5. Whether to seek medical care

**Definition.** The participant asks whether they should see a doctor or specialist, have a procedure, or contact their care team, or when to do so. The help wanted is a judgement on a specific decision about using professional care.

**Signs in the message:**

- "Should I see a medical specialist ... or try to wait it out?"
- "Would an endocrinologist benefit me"
- "Is it a must?" about a procedure
- Checking when they can or should call the care team

**Close call, vs. Ask what to do for relief:** This code is about a named decision to use (or not use) professional care, or its timing. If the person asks for self-care, medicine or habit steps, use Ask what to do for relief. A general question about what happens at an appointment is General health facts.

**Examples:**

- "Should I see a medical specialist about this or try to wait it out?"
- "Would an endocrinologist benefit me from learning why my hormones are our of whack?"

### R6. Personal plan or recommendations

**Definition.** The participant states personal wellness or fitness goals and asks for tailored recommendations, such as foods, meal ideas, supplements or a routine. The help wanted is a plan or product suggestion fitted to their goals, not treatment of a problem.

**Signs in the message:**

- States goals such as fat loss, muscle gain or toning
- Lists current diet, supplements, weight or budget
- "Please recommend specific foods" or "suggest ... ideas"
- "Is there a supplement or vitamin that has ..."

**Close call, vs. Ask what to do for relief:** This code is goal-driven planning for diet, supplements or routine. If the focus is easing or treating a current symptom or problem, use Ask what to do for relief.

**Examples:**

- "I want to discover affordable, healthy foods that will help me achieve my fitness and wellness goals."
- "fat loss, muscle gain and increasing testosterone are the main objectives."

## General close-call rules

- If a message describes the person's own symptoms *and* asks a general question, code both R1 and R2.
- R4 is only for messages with no question or request at all. Any stated question or request is coded instead.
- A question about what happens at a test or appointment is R2, not R5.
- 'Should I do X' questions that are not about using professional care are R3, not R5.
- A headache that is one of several flu-like symptoms is T5; a headache on its own is T9.
- Code what the person wrote. AI context (if shown) only helps you understand the message.
